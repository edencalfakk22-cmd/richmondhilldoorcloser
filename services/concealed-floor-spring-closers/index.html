A hidden closer is a design decision somebody made twenty or forty years ago, and it is one you inherit. On the all-glass entrances at Beaver Creek office condominiums, on the retail units along Highway 7 East and the Yonge corridor, and in professional-suite lobbies through town, nobody wanted a hydraulic box hanging over the door — so the mechanism went into the transom, into the door stile, or into a cement case set in the slab.

The trade-off arrives around year fifteen. A floor spring sits at grade in the exact line people walk, so every February it takes meltwater, brine and grit straight off boots, and the seal at the cover plate is all that stands between that and the mechanism. Once the seal lets go the case corrodes from the inside, and the symptoms follow in a predictable order: the leaf stops self-centring, the hold-open detent stops catching, oil appears around the plate, and finally the door drops because the pivot inside the spring has worn.

Servicing one is a different job from swapping a surface unit. The leaf comes off, the cover plate lifts, the mechanism comes out of the case, and the door usually needs re-shimming and re-aligning on its pivots when it goes back. Where the cement case is sound, that is the whole job. Where the case itself has gone, we say so before the door comes off and set out the options, rather than putting labour into a unit that will be back next winter.

FAQs

Floor Springs & Concealed Overhead Closers — your questions

The all-glass door to our suite no longer centres itself. What causes that?

The centring mechanism or the hold-open detent inside the floor spring has worn, or the spring has lost fluid. On an all-glass leaf there is not much else it can be — the patch fittings and the pivot are the whole assembly. It is a lift-the-door job rather than a valve adjustment, which is why it is worth doing before the leaf starts dragging as well.

Is a floor spring worth keeping, or should we move to a surface closer?

On a frameless glass entrance you generally have no choice: the leaf has no stile to mount anything to. On an aluminum door there is sometimes a real choice, and a surface closer is cheaper to maintain — at the cost of a visible box, which is exactly what the original designer was avoiding. We lay both options out rather than deciding for you.

Our building has floor springs at every professional-suite entrance. Can they be done together?

That is the sensible way. Doing several in one visit means one set-up, one lift of each leaf and consistent settings across the building, and we can tell you which cases are still sound and which are on borrowed time — so the replacements land in a planned budget rather than in the middle of February.
