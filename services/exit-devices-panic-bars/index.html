An exit device has one non-negotiable job: it releases the door in a single motion from the inside, with no key, no thumbturn and no special knowledge. Ontario's Building Code calls for panic hardware on exit doors serving an assembly floor area with an occupant load over one hundred, which in Richmond Hill covers a lot — school gyms and auditoriums, the community centres at Richmond Green, Oak Ridges and Bayview Hill, banquet halls and places of worship, and the larger restaurants along Highway 7 and the Yonge corridor.

The failure modes are predictable. Vertical-rod devices on double doors drift out of adjustment and the top latch stops engaging. Rim devices get dogged down permanently so the door never latches at all. Touch bars are bent by carts and deliveries, latches wear round and start slipping off the strike, and outside trim seizes after a winter of brine and grit. On a fire-rated opening the device must not be dogged at all, and labelled fire exit hardware has to stay labelled — we do not put non-rated parts onto a rated assembly to save a callback.

We repair and adjust rim, mortise and surface vertical-rod devices, replace worn latches, dogging mechanisms and outside trim, sort out electric strikes and delayed-egress arrangements where a building has them, and pair the device with a correctly set closer so the door latches cleanly instead of bouncing off the strike and standing open.

FAQs

Exit Devices & Panic Bars — your questions

Which Richmond Hill doors actually need panic hardware?

The Building Code requires it on exit doors serving an assembly floor area with an occupant load over one hundred — school gyms and auditoriums, community centres, halls, places of worship, larger restaurants and similar. Plenty of other commercial exits use exit devices by choice, because they are the most reliable way to guarantee one-motion egress.

Our exit door will not open from the inside. How fast can you come?

That is a life-safety emergency and we treat it as a priority dispatch anywhere in Richmond Hill and York Region. Do not chain, block or route people past it in the meantime — station someone at the door and call us, and we will get a technician out to make the opening work properly again.

Can we dog the panic bar down so the door stays unlatched during the day?

On a non-rated door, mechanical dogging is a normal feature and we will set it up properly. On a fire-rated door, no — fire exit hardware must not be dogged, because the door has to latch for the separation to hold. If that opening needs to stand open, a hold-open tied to the fire alarm is the correct solution.
