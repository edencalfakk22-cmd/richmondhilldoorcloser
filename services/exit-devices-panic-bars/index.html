Panic hardware exists for one moment that most buildings never have. Because of that, the way it fails is quiet. Nobody notices a top latch that stopped engaging in October, since the door still swings and the building still works. It only matters on the day a crowd is moving toward it in the dark.

Ontario’s Building Code requires panic hardware on exit doors serving an assembly floor area with an occupant load over one hundred. In Richmond Hill that pulls in the school gyms and auditoriums, the community centres at Richmond Green, Oak Ridges and Bayview Hill, banquet halls and places of worship, and the larger restaurants along Highway 7 and the Yonge corridor. Plenty of other exits carry devices by choice, which is a good instinct.

What we find, in rough order of frequency: vertical rods drifted out of adjustment so the top latch never picks up; rim devices dogged down permanently, which on a rated opening is not permitted at all; touch bars bent by carts; latches worn round and slipping off the strike; and outside trim seized after a winter of brine. We service rim, mortise and surface vertical-rod devices, replace worn parts rather than the whole chassis wherever the chassis is sound, keep labelled fire exit hardware labelled, and set the closer behind the device — because a device cannot latch a door the closer is not driving home.

FAQs

Exit Devices & Panic Bars — your questions

Our banquet hall’s exit doors get chained after the last event. Is that allowed?

Not while anyone is in the building, and it is one of the most serious findings there is. If the concern is after-hours entry, the fix is hardware — an exit device with the right keyed outside trim, an alarm on the door, or a delayed-egress arrangement where it is permitted — not a chain that somebody will forget to take off before the next booking.

The top latch on our double doors has stopped engaging. Does that matter?

Yes, on two counts. The pair no longer latches, so if it is a rated opening the separation has failed; and the inactive leaf can be pushed open, which people then start relying on. Vertical-rod devices drift out of adjustment as doors settle and frames move, and it is usually a rod adjustment or a worn top latch rather than a new device.

The outside trim on our exit door has seized. Can that be repaired on site?

Usually. Outside trim takes the worst of the weather — salt, grit and freeze-thaw all winter on an exposed plaza frontage — and it is a replaceable part on almost every device family. We free and service it where it is salvageable, and swap the trim without touching the chassis where it is not.
