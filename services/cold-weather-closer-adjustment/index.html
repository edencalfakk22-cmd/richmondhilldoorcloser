Closers are not a set-and-forget item in this climate. Cold thickens the hydraulic fluid, summer humidity swells the doors, and the taller buildings around Richmond Hill Centre and Langstaff generate real pressure differences across stairwell and corridor doors in winter. The same unit needs different settings in January than it does in July. Most of the emergency calls we take in the middle of a cold snap were visible weeks earlier as a door that was closing a little more slowly every day.

A maintenance visit is straightforward. We walk the doors, check every closer for fluid loss and arm or bracket damage, set sweep speed, latch speed and backcheck where the unit allows it, confirm each fire door still latches from any open position, and check opening force on barrier-free entrances so that a door someone made heavier to beat a draft has not quietly become one nobody can pull open. Cranking a spring up is the most common way that limit gets broken, and it is invisible until somebody complains.

Buildings with a lot of openings get far more out of one scheduled pass than out of six separate call-outs — condo and apartment towers, Beaver Creek office condominiums, plaza landlords with a strip of tenant entrances, schools working through the summer break. We book the pre-winter round from early autumn and leave a written list of what was adjusted and what needs replacing before the cold does it for us.

FAQs

Closing-Speed Adjustment & Preventive Maintenance — your questions

How often should commercial door closers be checked?

Twice a year suits most Richmond Hill buildings — once before the cold and once after it — with fire doors verified more often, because the Fire Code expects closures in a fire separation to be kept in working order and doors in a fire separation to be inspected monthly, not simply to have worked once.

Can our own maintenance staff adjust the closers?

The valves look simple, but sweep, latch and backcheck each control a different part of the swing, and backing one out too far dumps the remaining fluid onto the floor. If someone in-house is going to touch them, we will show them which valve does what while we are on site.

Why do all our doors get worse at the same time every winter?

Because two things hit them at once: colder fluid moving more slowly through the valves, and winter stack pressure pushing and pulling on doors in taller buildings. It is normal, it is predictable, and it is exactly why a seasonal adjustment round is worth booking before the first deep freeze.
