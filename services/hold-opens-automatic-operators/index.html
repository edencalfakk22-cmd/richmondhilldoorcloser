Two pieces of hardware get confused with each other constantly, and it is worth separating them, because they answer different questions.

A hold-open answers "this door needs to stand open for traffic." On a rated door there is exactly one acceptable version of that: a device wired to the fire alarm, so the door releases and self-closes on a signal. A magnetic wall holder qualifies, and so does a closer with an integral electromagnetic hold-open. A doorstop, a hook, a strap and a mechanical hold-open arm do not, because none of them releases. That distinction is invisible from the corridor, which is exactly why the wrong version survives in buildings for years.

A power operator answers a different question: "this door has to be usable by someone who cannot pull it." Low-energy push-plate operators on entrances, washrooms and accessible suites are the ones people notice, and they are the ones that quietly become a problem, because when the operator fails the door reverts to whatever the closer behind it is set at. If that closer was wound up over the years while the operator was masking it, the entrance stops being usable for precisely the person it was installed for.

We install and service both, plus what sits behind them: alarm interfaces, closer-holder combination units, push plates, wireless actuators, operator arms and control boxes — and the closer itself, so the door still behaves sensibly with the power off.

FAQs

Magnetic Hold-Opens & Barrier-Free Operators — your questions

What is the difference between a hold-open and an automatic operator?

A hold-open keeps a door open until something releases it, and on a rated door that release has to come from the fire alarm. An operator opens the door for you when a plate is pressed. They solve different problems, they are often on doors a few metres apart in the same building, and the only thing they share is that both still need a properly set closer behind them.

Our accessible washroom door is too heavy since the operator failed. Is that a compliance issue?

It can be. A door on a barrier-free path of travel has an opening-force limit, and a failed operator leaves the closer doing all the work — often a closer that was turned up over the years precisely because the operator was masking it. We repair the operator and set the manual force, so the door is usable either way.

Can a hold-open be added to a closer we already have?

Often, yes. Some closer families take an electromagnetic hold-open as an add-on; otherwise a separate wall or floor magnet does the job, wired back to the fire alarm panel. Either way the alarm connection is not optional on a rated door — an unreleased hold-open is just a wedge with better manners.
