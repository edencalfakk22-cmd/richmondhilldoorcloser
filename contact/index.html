Where we work in Richmond Hill

From Mill Pond, Bayview Hill, Oak Ridges, Jefferson, North Richvale, South Richvale, Crosby, Harding, Observatory, Rouge Woods, Langstaff and Beaver Creek Business Park, we cover every Richmond Hill neighbourhood — plus the surrounding York communities.

Fire doors in Richmond Hill condo and apartment common areas. Ontario's Fire Code is blunt about this: closures in a fire separation have to be maintained so they close and latch properly, and they must never be obstructed, blocked or wedged open. In practice that means the stairwell door, the garbage-chute room door, the parking-garage vestibule and every suite entrance in a Richmond Hill condo or rental building has to swing shut and latch by itself, from any open position, with nobody helping it. The Code also expects doors in a fire separation to be inspected monthly, which is exactly the kind of walk-through that turns up the same three findings in building after building — a closer that was disconnected years ago, a door that closes but rests against the strike without latching, and a wedge under a corridor door that somebody left there in the spring. Richmond Hill Fire and Emergency Services runs inspections through its Fire Prevention Division, so property managers here get asked to show that this work is being done, not just that the doors look fine.

Office entrances in Beaver Creek and the Highway 7 corridor. Beaver Creek Business Park sits in the corner of Richmond Hill closest to the 404 and 407, and the low-rise office condominiums along East and West Beaver Creek Road are full of the openings that eat closers: aluminum-and-glass entry doors on exposed corners, vestibules with an inner and outer door fighting each other, and demising doors into shared corridors that are also fire separations. Add the office and medical buildings along Highway 7 East and the towers going up around Richmond Hill Centre and Langstaff GO, and you have a lot of doors on a lot of maintenance contracts. Multi-tenant buildings are where a single badly set closer causes the most trouble — one entrance that slams gets reported by five tenants, and one that never latches leaves a shared corridor open to a stairwell.
